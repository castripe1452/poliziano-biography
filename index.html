A Renaissance Biography Project (1454–1494)

Scholar, Poet & Tutor to the Medici

Angelo Ambrogini, known to history as Poliziano (Politian), was the towering intellect of Lorenzo de’ Medici’s Laurentian circle. Translator of Homer, master of Latin and Greek philology, and pioneer of modern textual criticism, his vibrant Italian poetry redefined Renaissance humanism.

Chronological Narrative

The Life & Times of Poliziano

Explore key milestones in Poliziano's life, from his origins in Montepulciano to the height of his influence in Laurentian Florence.

1454
Montepulciano

Birth of Angelo Ambrogini

Born on July 14 in Montepulciano, Tuscany. Following the murder of his father, Benedetto, a lawyer devoted to the Medici family, young Angelo moved to Florence to seek education.

1470
Florence

Translation of Homer's Iliad

At just 16 years old, Poliziano began translating Books II–V of Homer’s Iliad into Latin hexameters. His mastery earned him entry into the household of Lorenzo "il Magnifico" de' Medici.

1475
Vernacular Poetry

Stanze per la Giostra

Composed for Giuliano de' Medici's jousting tournament victory. The poem stands as a masterpiece of Italian stanzaic verse, blending classical mythology with Neoplatonist ideals of beauty and love.

1478
Pazzi Conspiracy

Coniurationis Commentarium

Poliziano witnessed the violent Pazzi conspiracy in the Florence Cathedral, personally helping shield Lorenzo. He authored the official historical commentary on the conspiracy shortly thereafter.

1489
Philology Milestone

Publication of Miscellaneorum Centuria Prima

A groundbreaking work in modern textual analysis. Poliziano pioneered historical collation of manuscripts, establishing principles of textual genealogy still used by philologists today.

1494
Final Days

Death in Florence

Passed away on September 24, 1494, amidst the turmoil of Savonarola's influence and the French invasion of Italy. Interred at the Convent of San Marco in Florence.
